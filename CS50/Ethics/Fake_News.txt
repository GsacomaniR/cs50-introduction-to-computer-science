### 1. Which form of regulation is better, Proposal 1 or Proposal 2? Why?

**Proposal 1 is significantly better.**

While both proposals aim to combat misinformation, **Proposal 2 (outright blocking)** is a form of censorship that is fundamentally at odds with the principles of a democratic public sphere. It removes the user's ability to choose what to read and share, placing absolute authority in the hands of a third-party fact-checker and the platform's algorithm. This approach is paternalistic and risks creating a "Ministry of Truth" dynamic, where powerful entities get to unilaterally decide what is true. It also prevents users from ever seeing content that might be disputed but ultimately correct, or that they might want to be aware of, even if false, to understand a particular political discourse.

**Proposal 1 (warning labels)** is better because it respects user autonomy. It provides citizens with crucial information (an expert "nudge") but preserves their freedom to make their own judgment about the content. This approach aligns with Rini's argument, which focuses on informing and empowering users to be more responsible, not on creating a censorship regime. A warning label is a tool for accountability and transparency, serving as a flag that allows the public sphere to self-correct, rather than a gate that shuts down debate entirely.

**Important Context:** It is worth noting that this debate is not static. In January 2025, Meta (Facebook's parent company) announced it was discontinuing its third-party fact-checking program in the U.S. and replacing it with a "Community Notes" model, similar to X (formerly Twitter) . This new model is a form of **crowdsourced fact-checking**, which is different from both proposals in the lab. Early research suggests that such crowd-based efforts may be too slow to stop the viral spread of misinformation and can be subject to partisan bias . However, Meta argued its previous system led to "too many mistakes and too much censorship" . This recent change shows that the debate between expert-led and community-driven solutions is ongoing and complex.

### 2. Which proposal better preserves or promotes the five rights and opportunities for a democratic public sphere?

Based on the framework from Cohen and Fung , let's analyze each one:

**a. Which proposal better preserves or promotes Rights? Why?**

**Proposal 1 is better.** This proposal respects the right of users to access a wide range of information (even information that might be false) and their right to share it. It informs them but does not restrict their liberty. **Proposal 2**, by blocking content outright, directly violates the right to receive and impart information. It empowers the platform to act as a final arbiter of truth, which is a power no private or public entity should hold in a democracy.

**b. Which proposal better preserves or promotes the Opportunity for Expression? Why?**

**Proposal 1 is better.** The opportunity for expression is fundamental. **Proposal 2** completely eliminates this opportunity for any content judged as problematic. Proposal 1, on the other hand, preserves the opportunity for expression. A user can still post the article, and others can still share it. The only difference is that they must do so knowing it has been labeled as potentially false. Their expression is not suppressed; it is just contextualized.

**c. Which proposal better preserves or promotes Access? Why?**

**Proposal 1 is better.** Access means the ability for all citizens to obtain the information they need to participate in democratic life. Proposal 2 actually *denies* access to information, even if that information is widely available elsewhere or is part of a political narrative people need to understand. Proposal 1 preserves access: a warning label does not create a paywall or remove the post. Any citizen can still click, read, and decide for themselves, ensuring that access to the full range of political discourse is maintained.

**d. Which proposal better preserves or promotes Diversity? Why?**

**Proposal 1 is better.** A healthy public sphere requires diversity of voices and perspectives. Proposal 2 actively works against this by suppressing entire categories of viewpoints. While the goal is to suppress falsehoods, a centralized blocking system can easily be (and has been accused of being) biased against certain political perspectives, leading to a less diverse, more homogenous information environment . Proposal 1 allows a wider diversity of content to remain in the public square. The warning label provides a signal about quality, but it does not eliminate the diversity of thought, allowing citizens to be exposed to a wider range of ideas.

**e. Which proposal better preserves or promotes the Power Communicative? Why?**

**Proposal 1 is better.** "Power Communicative" refers to the ability of citizens' arguments, not just their wealth or status, to influence public opinion. A warning label adds to the *communicative power* of the fact-checkers and the platform's system. However, crucially, it does not destroy the communicative power of the individual user who posts the content. The user can still use the post to make a point, even if it is contested. **Proposal 2** is a blunt instrument that destroys the communicative power of the user by rendering their speech inaudible. Furthermore, it arguably harms the public's communicative power by restricting the information available for debate, thereby weakening the overall quality of the democratic deliberation.
